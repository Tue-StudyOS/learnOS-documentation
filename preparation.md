# Appendix: Interview Guides and Methodology

## 1. Methodology

We explored how students prepare for exams and how teaching staff support learning and identify difficulties in understanding.

We conducted qualitative interviews using open questions and follow-up questions adapted to each conversation. Inspired by *The Mom Test*, we focused on existing practices: concrete experiences, difficulties and tools already in use. 

We interviewed eight students from German studies, medical engineering, psychology, economics and computer science through existing contacts (convenience sampling).

We grouped recurring observations into themes, particularly “what to study” and “how to study”, and used these to identify requirements and revise the project idea. Conversations with teaching staff added perspectives on feedback about student understanding, exercise sessions and practical conditions for adoption.

This small, non-representative sample provides design insights, not evidence of effectiveness. The guides below have been condensed and consolidated for this appendix.

## 2. Interview Guide: Students

### Core questions

1. How did you prepare for your last three exams?
2. What was most annoying about exam preparation?
3. What tools or materials did you use?
4. How do you decide what is relevant for an exam?

### Supplementary questions

5. How do you organise your study materials and study time?
6. When do you notice gaps in your knowledge or recurring mistakes?
7. How does your approach vary by subject and exam format?
8. What do you use AI for when studying, and what makes it difficult to use?
9. What role do studying with others and your study environment play?
10. What support is missing from your exam preparation?

## 3. Interview Guide: Teaching Side

### Questions for Lecturers

1. What do you see as the main purpose of a lecture?
2. What challenges do you currently encounter in teaching?
3. How do you organise exercises and tutorials to help students achieve the learning objectives?
4. How did you create your most recent exercise sheet? How long did it take, and where were the bottlenecks?
5. How do you currently receive feedback on students’ understanding?
6. What feedback on students’ understanding would you find useful?
7. How would you use this feedback in your teaching?
8. Who else would you recommend we speak to about these topics?

### Questions for tutoring projects

These questions extend the teaching perspective to a specific project context.

9. What problem is your AI tutor; PhyKo-Chat intended to solve?
10. What data can you provide, and what inputs and outputs would you need for integration?
11. Would a separate program to support tutorial sessions be useful to you?
